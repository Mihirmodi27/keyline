# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Software engineers, engineering managers and prospective hires who arrive at a single Field Notes article from a link (a newsletter, Hacker News, LinkedIn, a colleague's chat message). They read on a laptop at a desk or on a phone between meetings, usually in one sitting of five to ten minutes. Many are evaluating whether the approach in the post applies to their own system; some are quietly evaluating whether Halden is a place they would want to work. The job to be done: understand what the team did, see the evidence (code, numbers, the mistakes), and leave with something they can reuse.

## Product Purpose

Field Notes is the engineering blog of Halden, a European parcel logistics company. It publishes first-person accounts from the people who build Halden's logistics platform. It exists to share hard-won engineering lessons in public, to build the platform team's reputation, and to support hiring. Success is a reader who finishes the article, trusts the numbers, follows a related post or subscribes to the fortnightly email, and remembers that Halden's engineers are careful and candid.

## Brand Personality

Candid, precise, unhurried. The voice is a senior engineer explaining a project to a peer: plain sentences, real numbers, named mistakes, no hype. The page should feel modern and polished (the client's words) in the way a well-run operation feels polished: everything in its place, nothing performing. Emotional goal: quiet confidence and trust. The reader should feel the team knows what it is doing because it shows its working.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS marketing blog templates: gradient hero, centered stack, a grid of identical cards, stock "growth" language.
- Content-marketing pages that bury the article under share bars, pop-ups, newsletter modals and sticky banners.
- Developer-costume styling: neon-on-black terminals, monospace used everywhere to signal "technical".
- Anything that looks like it was made by an AI page generator without a point of view.

## Design Principles

1. **The article is the product.** Every element on the page either helps someone read the post or gets out of the way. Chrome (header, share, contents, footer) stays quiet.
2. **Show the working.** Code, tables and numbers are first-class content and get the same care as prose; they are evidence, not decoration.
3. **Calm over clever.** Polish reads as order and consistency, not as effects. If a flourish draws attention to itself rather than the content, cut it.
4. **Logistics precision.** Things line up, numbers align, labels are consistent. The page should feel as dependable as a scan event.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor: 4.5:1 for body and UI text, 3:1 for large text and non-text indicators, visible keyboard focus everywhere, a working skip link. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (the animated cover and smooth scrolling stop) and `prefers-color-scheme` (light and dark both supported and both meeting AA). Readers include non-native English speakers, so body text stays large with a comfortable measure. Touch targets on phones are at least 44 by 44 px.
