# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

People who organise meetings for remote and distributed teams: team leads, engineering and design managers, operations and customer-success staff who book calls across three or more time zones every week. They arrive on this page from a search, a referral or a colleague's invite, usually on a laptop during the working day, sometimes on a phone between calls. The job they want done: stop spending hours on "does 4pm your time work?" threads, and stop making the same teammates take every early or late call.

## Product Purpose

Tandem reads a team's calendars and working hours across time zones and suggests the meeting time that costs nobody their evening, then books it. This landing page exists to explain that in one screen, show the product doing it, prove that real teams use it, answer the pricing and privacy questions, and get the visitor to start free (Free plan up to 5 people; Team at $8 per user per month; Business at $16). Success is a visitor who understands the overlap idea from the hero visual alone and clicks "Start free".

## Brand Personality

Fair, calm, exact. The voice is plain and specific: it names real cities, real hours and real numbers instead of adjectives. It should leave the visitor feeling relief (someone else does the time-zone arithmetic) and trust (it only reads free/busy, it treats everyone's evening the same). The client asked for "modern and polished"; the lane is tech-minimal product marketing, where the product visual carries the story and the type does the rest.

## Anti-references

- The generic AI-built SaaS landing page: centred stack of eyebrow, headline and subhead repeated in every section; identical icon-over-heading feature cards; soft orange and blue gradient blobs behind the hero; everything wrapped in a bordered rounded card.
- The hero-metric template (big number, small label, gradient accent).
- Decorative motion that loops forever (floating cards, pulsing dots).
- Glassmorphism used as decoration.

## Design Principles

1. Show, don't tell: the timeline visual is the argument; copy only points at what it shows.
2. Fairness is the brand: no element should look more important than its job, the way no teammate's evening is more important than another's.
3. Exact over impressive: real times, real cities, real prices; no vague superlatives.
4. Quiet confidence: one accent, used where it means "the chosen time" or "act here".
5. Nothing in a box unless it is a thing you pick (a plan) or a thing you would screenshot (the app).

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: body text at least 4.5:1, large text and UI boundaries at least 3:1, visible keyboard focus on every control in light and dark sections. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Colour is never the only signal in the product visual (cells carry hour numbers and the legend names each state), which matters for colour-blind readers. Touch targets at least 44px on coarse pointers. Audience is international: dates, times and UTC offsets are written unambiguously.
